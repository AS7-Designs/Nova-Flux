Generative AI moved from keynote demos to shipping features across every major CRM platform in the first half of 2025. Here is an honest look at what is production-ready and what is still roadmap.

## Salesforce: Einstein GPT and Agentforce

Salesforce pushed AI agents that can:

- Summarize account histories before meetings
- Draft emails grounded in CRM data
- Execute multi-step workflows (schedule meetings, update records, create tasks)

**Reality check:** Summarization and drafting work well with clean data. Autonomous agents still require careful guardrails — most enterprises run in human-in-the-loop mode.

## HubSpot: AI-powered content and deal intelligence

HubSpot focused on accessibility for mid-market teams:

- AI blog and email generation tied to CRM segments
- Deal insights surfacing risk factors from activity patterns
- Conversation intelligence included in higher tiers

**Reality check:** Strong for marketing-sales alignment. Less depth on complex enterprise forecasting.

## Microsoft: Copilot for Dynamics 365

Microsoft leveraged its Office footprint:

- Outlook and Teams context feeding Dynamics records
- Meeting prep briefs from email + CRM + SharePoint
- Power Automate integration for AI-triggered workflows

**Reality check:** Best fit for orgs already deep in Microsoft 365. Cross-platform teams may hit friction.

## Independent vendors: speed vs. depth

Best-of-breed AI sales tools (conversation intelligence, forecasting, outreach) continue to outpace native CRM AI on specific workflows — but integration tax is real.

## What buyers should ask

1. **Where does the model run?** Data residency and training data policies matter.
2. **What data does it access?** Principle of least privilege for AI agents.
3. **Can reps override?** Human approval workflows for customer-facing output.
4. **How is it priced?** Per-seat AI add-ons can double CRM costs quickly.

## Bottom line

Gen AI in CRM is real — but uneven. Summarization and drafting are mature. Autonomous agents are emerging. The winners will be teams that match vendor capabilities to their actual workflow gaps, not the flashiest demo.
